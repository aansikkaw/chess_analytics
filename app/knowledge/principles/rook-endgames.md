# Why they matter

Rook endgames are the most common endgame type in practice, so small improvements here pay off in many games.[^dvoretsky-manual]

# Essentials

* **Activity over material.** An active rook is often worth a pawn. A passive rook tied to defence loses many
  endgames that look equal.
* **Rooks belong behind passed pawns** (the Tarrasch rule): behind your own passer to push it, behind the enemy
  passer to stop it.
* **Cut off the enemy king** along a rank or file; distance from the passed pawn decides many endings.
* **Know the Lucena position** (how the stronger side wins with a pawn on the seventh, by "building a bridge") and
  the **Philidor position** (how the weaker side draws by holding the third rank, then checking from behind).[^villa-100]

# Habit

Before every move in a rook endgame, ask: is my rook active? Can I make it more active?
