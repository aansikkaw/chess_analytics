# Principle

* [Activate the king in the endgame](king-activity.md) - Once queens are off and the mating danger is gone, the king becomes a strong fighting piece.
* [Blunder check before every move](blunder-check.md) - A short checklist that catches most one-move blunders: what does my move leave undefended, and what does it allow?
* [Clock management](clock-management.md) - Budget time so the critical moments get it; avoid reaching move 30 with too little time to calculate.
* [Converting a winning position](converting-advantages.md) - When you are clearly better, reduce counterplay first; simplification and safety usually beat speed.
* [Defending worse positions](defending-worse-positions.md) - Stubborn defence saves points: create problems, seek fortresses, and avoid collapsing in one move.
* [Keeping a repertoire healthy](repertoire-maintenance.md) - A repertoire is only useful if you remember it, it covers what opponents actually play, and it leads to positions you understand.
* [Look at forcing moves first](forcing-moves-first.md) - Checks, captures and threats, for both sides, are where tactics live; examine them before quiet moves.
* [Middlegame planning](middlegame-planning.md) - Find a plan from the position's imbalances: pawn structure, weak squares, piece activity and king safety.
* [Opening principles](opening-principles.md) - Development, centre control and king safety, and why understanding beats memorising long lines.
* [Recognise mating patterns](mating-patterns.md) - Knowing the common mating patterns by sight lets you spot forced mates quickly, for both sides.
* [Rook endgame essentials](rook-endgames.md) - Active rooks, rooks behind passed pawns, cutting off the king, and the two theoretical positions every player should know.
